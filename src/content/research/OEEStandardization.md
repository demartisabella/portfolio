## Research Overview

Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE) is only meaningful when production planning reflects the actual operational capacity of a manufacturing system. This work addresses the gap between theoretical production schedules and real shop-floor execution by redesigning planning logic and standardizing operational procedures within a FESTO flexible manufacturing cell.

## Problem

The original production plan assumed 22 hours of weekly availability and a production capacity that operators could not realistically sustain. This mismatch prevented reliable OEE measurement because planned production time no longer represented actual operating conditions.

## Methodology

The study combined:

- Time studies
- Capacity analysis
- Production balancing
- Operational standardization
- Lean Manufacturing principles

to redesign the production schedule around the real operating constraints of the manufacturing cell.

## Key Contributions

- Developed a standardized production planning methodology for OEE implementation.
- Reduced the gap between theoretical and effective production capacity.
- Established the operational conditions required for meaningful OEE measurement.
- Created the foundation for future digital monitoring and analytics.

## Engineering Value

This work became the first stage of a broader Industry 4.0 research initiative focused on digital manufacturing at the SIMTEC Advanced Manufacturing Laboratory. Its results later enabled the development of an automated Microsoft Power Platform ecosystem for real-time OEE analysis.

Beyond improving production planning, this work established the operational foundations required for digital manufacturing initiatives. The standardized process later enabled the development of an automated OEE ecosystem based on Microsoft Power Platform.
