## Research Overview

Implementing a digital platform is only valuable if the resulting information is reliable. This research evaluates the effectiveness of the proposed OEE ecosystem by measuring improvements in data consistency, repeatability, and analytical reliability through statistical validation.

## Problem

The original manual OEE measurement workflow relied on paper-based records and delayed spreadsheet transcription, resulting in a 43.43% global data inconsistency rate. This lack of traceability and high error rate directly distorted the calculated OEE values, meaning operational decisions were being made on an unreliable and unsustainable representation of system performance.

## Methodology

The ecosystem was evaluated through:

- Global inconsistency analysis
- Two-proportion hypothesis testing
- Paired OEE comparison
- Sensitivity analysis


# Key Results
- Reduced inconsistencies from 43.43% to 2.25%
- Improved repeatability
- Increased traceability
- Standardized programmed production time
- Produced statistically reliable OEE indicators

## Engineering Value

Rather than focusing solely on automation, this work demonstrates that successful Industry 4.0 initiatives require trustworthy manufacturing data. By statistically validating the digital ecosystem, the research establishes a repeatable framework for reliable production analytics and data-driven decision-making.
