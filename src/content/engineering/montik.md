## Project Overview

This project applied the DMAIC methodology to improve the dough portioning operation at Montik Bakery. The objective was to reduce process time while maintaining product quality through engineering analysis rather than trial-and-error modifications.

## Problem

Manual dough portioning represented the primary production bottleneck. The process required excessive operator effort, generated variability between portions, and limited daily production capacity.

## Engineering Approach

The improvement process followed the complete DMAIC cycle:

- Process mapping
- Time studies
- Root cause analysis
- Statistical validation
- FlexSim simulation
- Engineering concept generation
- Economic evaluation

Several improvement alternatives were generated and compared before selecting the final solution.


## Key Results

- Projected **40% reduction** in dough portioning time.
- Maintained product quality requirements.
- Validated improvements through simulation before implementation.
- Demonstrated positive economic feasibility.

## Impact

The project illustrates how combining statistical analysis with discrete-event simulation supports engineering decision-making before investing in physical implementation. The methodology can be replicated in similar food manufacturing environments seeking productivity improvements with minimal capital investment.

## Lessons Learned

This project strengthened my ability to integrate statistical analysis, industrial engineering methodologies, and business decision-making into a single improvement initiative. Beyond applying Lean Six Sigma tools, I learned how to validate measurement systems before drawing conclusions, quantify process variation using hypothesis testing, compare engineering alternatives through simulation and economic evaluation, and translate analytical findings into practical solutions that balance operational performance, implementation cost, and long-term process sustainability.
