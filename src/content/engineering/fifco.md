## Project Overview

Following FIFCO's sale of its beverage and retail divisions to Heineken, the remaining company faced a fundamentally different financial structure. This project evaluated whether the continuing real estate and hospitality business generated sufficient economic value to justify future investment. Rather than focusing solely on accounting indicators, the study applied engineering economic analysis to determine the true profitability of the remaining operation.


## Problem

Following the sale of its beverages, food, and retail divisions, FIFCO transitioned toward a business focused primarily on real estate and hospitality. The project assessed whether the remaining business generated sufficient long-term value despite maintaining strong financial health.

## Engineering Approach

The evaluation included:

- Financial statement analysis
- Debt restructuring analysis
- Net Cash Flow (FNE) model
- Net Present Value (NPV or VPN)
- Internal Rate of Return (IRR or TIR)
- Minimum Attractive Rate of Return (MARR or TMAR)
- Sensitivity analysis
- Liquidity, leverage, and coverage ratio evaluation

Future cash flows were projected over a fifteen-year horizon using multiple growth scenarios.


## Key Results

- Estimated a TMAR of 9.8% using inflation, business risk, and market discount rate assumptions.
- Developed a 15-year discounted cash flow model based on projected Free Net Cash Flow (FNE).
- Determined an NPV of −₡61.23 billion and an IRR of −7.21%, indicating the investment failed to meet the required return threshold.
- Conducted sensitivity analysis across multiple growth and discount-rate scenarios, confirming that the investment remained economically unattractive under all realistic assumptions.
- Demonstrated how engineering economic analysis can support objective capital investment decisions beyond traditional accounting metrics.

## Impact

This project demonstrates the application of engineering economics to strategic corporate decision-making. Rather than relying solely on accounting performance, it combines cash flow modeling, discounting techniques, investment evaluation, and sensitivity analysis to support objective investment decisions under uncertainty.

The methodology can be generalized to manufacturing investments, capital equipment purchases, infrastructure projects, and corporate valuation studies.

## Lessons Learned

This analysis highlighted that financial viability and economic attractiveness are fundamentally distinct. Evaluating major corporate transactions requires a multi-dimensional approach, combining operational, financial, and strategic lenses. Ultimately, applying rigorous sensitivity analysis and engineering economics provides the objective framework needed to validate long-term investment decisions under uncertainty.
