## Project Overview

This project applied Operations Research techniques to evaluate the performance of a three-server service system. By combining statistical validation with queueing theory, the study quantified customer waiting times, system utilization, and the operational impact of increasing service capacity.

## Problem

Barbería Baruch experienced significant customer queues during peak demand periods, resulting in long waiting times and customer abandonment. Management lacked objective data to determine whether additional service capacity was justified.


## Engineering Approach

The study combined industrial engineering and statistical methods, including:

- Time and motion studies
- Levene's Test
- ANOVA
- Shapiro-Wilk Normality Test
- ILO Standard Time methodology
- M/M/3 Queueing Models
- Sensitivity Analysis using POM-QM
- Data processing in Excel and Minitab

Customer arrivals were analyzed by hour to distinguish normal and peak operating conditions before constructing mathematical queueing models.

## Key Results

- Determined standard service times for three service categories.
- Calculated hourly arrival and service rates.
- Identified system utilization of approximately **95%** with three barbers.
- Estimated average customer waiting time of approximately **38 minutes** during peak periods.
- Demonstrated that adding a fourth barber would reduce utilization to **69%** while decreasing average waiting time to approximately **11 minutes**.

## Impact

The analysis transformed subjective operational concerns into quantitative decision-making. Rather than recommending additional staffing based on intuition, the project demonstrated—with mathematical evidence—the operational benefits of increasing service capacity during peak demand.

## Lessons Learned

This study reinforced that queueing systems become highly unstable as utilization approaches 100%, making rigorous statistical validation essential before applying mathematical models. I learned that effective capacity planning requires balancing operational efficiency with customer experience. Notably, the analysis demonstrated that even small, targeted increases in capacity can generate disproportionately large reductions in waiting times.
